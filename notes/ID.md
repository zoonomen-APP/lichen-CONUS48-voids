apex_place: Priest River Experimental Forest, Bonner County, Idaho
apex_mechanism: one field season (1969), with duplicates

Idaho's apex cell lies in or beside the Priest River Experimental Forest, a Forest Service research forest in Bonner County, Idaho, and it is mostly the record of one summer. Theodore L. Esslinger collected 815 of the cell's 896 records between June and August 1969, on about forty days. One of his labels (WIS-L-0119503) names the forest and gives the forest habitat type, "Tsuga-Pachistima", which suggests an ecological study rather than general collecting.

Most of the records stand on two points a couple of kilometres apart, both given in whole minutes of latitude and longitude, as his labels give them: 408 records on one and 338 on the other. Apart from Esslinger the cell is thin. McCune, Rosentreter, Spribille and others added about 50 records between 1997 and 2004.

Esslinger's 824 records are not 824 collections. They are spread across about twenty herbaria, and counted by date and taxon they come to about 500 gatherings, many of them held two to twelve times as duplicates. The largest shares are in his own herbarium and at the University of Idaho (ID), and those two mostly hold different gatherings. The apex therefore reflects one intensive field season, enlarged by the distribution of its duplicates.

The cell's third most frequent taxon, *Esslingeriana idahoensis*, belongs to a genus named in Esslinger's honour. Its type apparently came not from the cell but from Mount Spokane State Park, Spokane County, Washington, collected by Esslinger in 1970, the year after his Priest River season.
