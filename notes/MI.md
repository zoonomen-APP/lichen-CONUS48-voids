apex_place: Isle Royale, Keweenaw County, Michigan
apex_mechanism: one collector's repeated visits, chiefly 1983

Michigan's apex cell (its densest hexagon) holds 3,270 records, all on Isle Royale in Lake Superior: every record lies between 48.03 and 48.15 N and between 88.56 and 88.78 W, the middle of the island, which is part of Keweenaw County, Michigan, and of Isle Royale National Park. C. M. Wetmore collected 2,215 of them (68%). The island's long, narrow shape may add to the concentration, since collecting spread along it can fall into only a few cells of this grid.

Wetmore's records are not one campaign. 594 are from 1957 to 1959, 149 from a single day in 1972, 88 from 1980, 1,050 from 1983 (mostly July, nearly all held at MIN), 225 from 1984, and 105 from 1999. The 1,363 records the block gives for the 1980s are essentially the summer of 1983.

Others came in episodes. In 1930 a party of A. H. Povah, C. A. Brown, J. B. McFarlin and J. L. Lowe collected 351 records between July and September, 289 of them now at MICH. On 1972-08-25 seven named collectors (Wetmore, S. C. Tucker, J. W. Thomson, T. H. Nash, R. S. Egan, P. K. Armstrong and L. Tibell) left 366 records, most of them at two points near 48.12 N, 88.57 W, and their specimens went to several herbaria (MIN, LSU, WIS, ASU and others); the records do not say what the occasion was. J. A. Malachowski collected 459 in 1976 and 1977, nearly all held at MSC.

The taxa table should be read with Malachowski in mind. Its five leading names are all Usnea, and 384 of the cell's Usnea records are his, from June and July 1977. The table reflects one collector's focus more than the island's lichens.
