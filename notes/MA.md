The densest cell in Massachusetts is not where one would guess. It sits
in Norfolk County, in the region of the town of Dover and near Wellesley College. 

Clara Cummings collected extensively in this region, but she died in the
early twentieth century. So to the extent her work drives this cell, the
density is not the result of her collecting alone — it is the result of
subsequent geolocation work, of someone much later assigning latitudes and
longitudes to her records. Material of hers that was never georeferenced
does not appear on this map at all.

The summary reveals problems in the data set. Cummings was not collecting in 
1853, that record almost certainly is 1883.
Her name is not uniformly represented (though in the 383 instances has been
regularized by me).

That makes this apex region and count a useful reminder about what these sheets show. A bright
cell records where specimens have been collected, accessioned, databased,
and given coordinates. The last of those steps happened a century after the
first, and it happened unevenly.

Massachusetts also has no unattested cells at this grain — every cell in
the state holds at least one record. That is a statement about an 18.2 km
grid as much as about the collecting, and it sits oddly beside the
widespread belief that the state is picked over and no longer worth
working.
