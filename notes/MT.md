Montana's densest cell sits just west of Bozeman, 888 records.

The apex results from a master's thesis. Jessie Salix surveyed Lewis and Clark
Caverns State Park for her degree at Montana State, collecting from
twenty-two sites and finding 164 species — almost half on rock, a third on
bark or wood. She holds 793 of the cell's 888 records, all between 2000
and 2011, against 95 for everyone else combined. The park is small enough
to sit inside a single cell at this resolution, so a survey that would be
invisible at a coarser grain becomes the brightest cell in the state.

The taxa are dry-country saxicolous: Physcia caesia, Xanthoparmelia
mexicana, Aspicilia cinerea, Lobothallia alphoplaca. Rock lichens on the
eastern slope, not the forest flora that dominates Montana's western
sheets.
