apex_place: Santa Rosa Island, Santa Barbara County
apex_mechanism: two inventories

California's densest cell lies on Santa Rosa Island, in Channel Islands National Park. It holds 2,400 records from 42 collectors. Almost all of them come from two short periods: 1,343 records from the 1990s, nearly all from 1993-1994, and 1,020 from the 2000s. Before 1990 the cell has two dated records.

The first period is an Arizona State University party: Nash, Ryan, Wetmore and Marsh account for 1,234 records, most of them dated 1994. Stephen and Sylvia Sharnoff were on the island the same year, collecting the voucher specimens behind the photographs in Lichens of North America; those are preserved specimens, most of them at the Canadian Museum of Nature, entered under several spellings of the pair's names. The second is the National Park's own lichen inventory, carried out with the UC Riverside herbarium: Knudsen has 890 records, nearly all from 2006-2009, and Kindsvater's 106 from 2003-2004 are at Santa Barbara Botanic Garden.

The taxa read as an inventory with fog-zone coast taxa. No name reaches 2% of the records; the ten most frequent are each taken between 30 and 41 times, and they include Niebla, Vermilacinia, Teloschistes flavicans and Leucodermia leucomelos. The cell is dense; an island is a bounded thing to inventory and a boat trip concentrates the collectors.
