Washington's apex cell: the longer story

Companion to the short note beside Washington's sheet. Begun 2026-11-02; added to as understanding changes, with each addition dated. Counts are from clh_2026_08 as of 2026-11-02 (cell 94620, 1,599 records), after Jim Riley's names were regularized to Riley,J. the same day.

The history told here is inferred from the CLH records alone: dates, collectors, herbaria, coordinates and catalogue numbers, read as they stand in the snapshot. Other sources, such as the accession records of WTU and OSC, Forest Service project reports, publications, or the recollections of people who were there, may add to it or alter it, and could show parts of it to be false.

Where

The cell lies almost entirely in Lewis County, Washington (1,588 records); 10 records fall just over the line in Skamania County, Washington, and one has no county. Every record is between 46.41 and 46.59 N and between 121.68 and 121.90 W, on 93 distinct points. The short note places it in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, between Mount Rainier, Mount Adams and Mount St. Helens; that comes from Alan's knowledge of the area, not from these files.

One site, one autumn

887 of the 1,599 records (55%) sit on a single point, 46.488, -121.8098. All 887 are held at WTU. 880 of them are from 2000, the rest from 2001; the first is dated 2000-08-29 and the last 2001-09-28. The work came on 21 dates, mostly in September 2000: the busiest were 2000-09-28 (123 records), 09-11 (111), 09-14 (83), 10-03 (75) and 10-17 (69). For the cell as a whole, 2000 brought 951 records: 94 in August, 707 in September, 148 in October.

Seven collectors share the point: J. Ricklefs 548, Chris O'Connell 159, Vince Harke 67, Elizabeth Hoecker 59, Amy Freestone 31, Helen Lau 13, Scott Johnson 10. Every record these seven have in the cell is on this point. Under these names, Chris O'Connell's 159 and Vince Harke's 67 are their whole presence in CLH, and Elizabeth Hoecker's 97 are all in Washington (checked 2026-11-02).

What the records show is a crew returning again and again to one coordinate over about seven weeks. That reads like work at a study site rather than a survey spread across the landscape, but the records can't separate two possibilities: a single site, or a whole study area whose specimens were all given one coordinate. The coordinate itself, written to three and four decimals and shared by every specimen, is a site or area coordinate, not a reading taken specimen by specimen. 51 of the 887 records have no catalogue number. What brought the crew there, the records don't say, and Alan doesn't know (2026-11-02).

2026-11-02: On Google Earth (imagery dated 2026-06-01), the point falls in a patch of tall trees surrounded by logged blocks of varying age (Alan's reading of the image). The imagery shows the forest now, not in 2000.

The OSC visits

Nearly everything else in the cell is at OSC, and it came in separate visits by other people. 1994 and 1995: S. D. Berryman 99, with Jim Riley, Linda Geiser 25 and E. Phenix 17. 2004: J. Grenon 51, Geiser 32 and D. Glavich 31, at three separate points. 2014: Amanda Hardman 149, on six points. Riley's 144 records run from 1992 to 2014, 109 of them at OSC, and include 9 of the 10 Skamania County records near the cell's southern edge (1994 to 2004). Geiser's 57 run from 1994 to 2004.

Earlier and later

October 1972, mostly 1972-10-21: D. E. Anderegg (alone and with others) and L. H. Pike collected together; 31 records from 1972, held at ID (19), LD (4), UME (2), OSC (2) and singly at ASU, IDS, O and WIS, which suggests duplicates sent out (inferred). 1993: the Sharnoffs, 23 records, 21 of them at CMN. 1999 and 2013: Bruce McCune, 10 records, mostly OSC. 2021: Garry Neil, 29 records in September and October, all at ASU.

The taxa table

The ten leading taxa account for 764 records, and 615 of them (80%) come from the one site in 2000 and 2001. Cladonia ochrochlora, at the head with 204, is 201 from that site; Platismatia glauca 96 of 118; Hypogymnia apinnata 53 of 63. So the table describes what one crew found at one place, more than the cell's forest. It is the same lesson as Colorado's apex (S. Shushan's distributed gatherings of 1961-07-03) and Michigan's (J. A. Malachowski's Usnea of 1977), here through a site rather than a person.

Open questions

What was the 2000 crew's project, and who ran it? Is 46.488, -121.8098 one site, or a study area given one coordinate? Why do 51 of its records lack catalogue numbers? What linked the OSC visits of 1994 to 1995, 2004 and 2014: one program returning, or separate projects? What brought Anderegg and Pike in October 1972?
