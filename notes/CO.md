apex_place: Middle Boulder Creek Canyon, Boulder County, Colorado
apex_mechanism: one collector's day, distributed as duplicates

Colorado's apex cell (its densest hexagon) holds 1,500 records, and most come from one spot: Middle Boulder Creek Canyon, about 1.5 miles northeast of Tungsten in the vicinity of Castle Rock, Boulder County, Colorado, at about 2,410 m, as three specimen labels give it. Sam Shushan collected 601 of the 1,500 (40%), between 1952 and 1987, and the 1960s alone account for 756 records. W. A. Weber, often Shushan's companion in the field in the 1950s, follows with 127; his many name forms were regularized on 2026-10-22, before the block below was drawn.

The mechanism is a single date. 352 records carry Shushan's name and the date 1961-07-03, held by 37 herbaria; 305 of them fall in this cell. They bear his own collector numbers (mostly written "sl-"), 108 distinct, and the numbers are of two kinds: 74 are held by one herbarium only, while eight are held by 15 to 18 herbaria each. Those eight were gathered in quantity and sent out as duplicates. None of the three labels read names a series, so this is not an exsiccata; it is one collector's day of numbered gatherings, some of them distributed widely. Whether every one was collected that day, and how many organisms a number stands for, the records cannot say.

The taxa table should be read with that in mind. Its leading names (Parmelia saxatilis, Nephroma parile, Leptogium saturninum, Peltigera aphthosa, P. collina) are largely the taxa of the most widely distributed numbers; number 172, Parmelia saxatilis, alone has 31 records in 18 herbaria. So the table weights those taxa by what Shushan chose to send out, as well as by what grew there. Each specimen still stands on its own: two of the numbers already carry different names in different herbaria, and Jacob Watts, the cell's fourth collector (2023 to 2024), has redetermined at least one of the 1961 packets at COLO in 2026. The cell also ranks second of 479 CONUS48 hexagons for Umbilicaria hyperborea, with 44 records (only a cell in Coos County, New Hampshire, holds more, 58), and 20 of those 44 are Shushan's from 1961-07-03. The cell's earlier history is smaller: 75 records from the 1890s, 41 of them Arthur E. Morgan's in 1898.
